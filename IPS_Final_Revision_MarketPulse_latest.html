Cash-flow statement and the conflicting demands on cashCollectionAnalysis

GPFM Ch 3 · Cash Flow Demands and Conflicts

Inflows (salary, business income, rent, interest, dividends) minus outflows, split into fixed (EMI, rent, insurance premium, school fees — contractual, cannot be cut quickly), variable (food, fuel, utilities) and discretionary (travel, dining, gadgets). The difference is net cash flow = the amount available to save or invest.

ConflictWhat FPSB expects you to say
Save for retirement vs pay for children's educationRetirement cannot be funded by a loan; education can — prioritise retirement, use education loans if needed
Repay debt vs investCompare after-tax cost of debt with after-tax expected return; high-cost unsecured debt (cards, personal loans) is repaid first, always
Emergency fund vs investEmergency fund first — 3 to 6 months of expenses in liquid assets before any goal-based investing
Insurance premium vs investmentAdequate pure protection (term, health) precedes wealth creation; an under-insured household's plan fails on the first shock
Present consumption vs future goalsBudget first, then automate savings ("pay yourself first")
The FPSB priority ladder, used in every synthesis question: Emergency fund → Insurance → High-cost debt → Goal investing → Tax optimisation → Estate. If an answer option skips a rung, it is wrong however attractive its return.
Depreciation, notional rent and "unrealised gains" are not cash flows. A statement that includes them is a profit-and-loss statement, not a cash-flow statement. Likewise, loan principal repayment is a cash outflow even though it is not an expense.

Financial management strategies — developing, optimising, implementingSynthesis

GPFM Ch 6 · the "what would the planner recommend first" chapter

The strategy set FPSB lists: increase income, reduce/ re-prioritise expenses, restructure or refinance debt (consolidate high-cost into low-cost, shorten tenure when affordable), build reserves, automate savings, review insurance, use tax-advantaged vehicles, and align asset ownership with goals. Each is judged on advantages vs disadvantages and then optimised (the combination with the best goal impact at acceptable behavioural cost) before an implementation plan with prioritised, dated action steps.

The three-step process FPSB names in every module: 1. Collection (quantitative + qualitative data) → 2. Analysis (ratios, gaps, constraints, opportunities) → 3. Synthesis and recommendation (integrated plan, prioritised actions, monitoring). Inflation, serial payments, PVAD of a serial paymentAnalysishighest-yield TVM

GPFM Ch 2 · Intermediate TVM · 6-6, 6-7

A serial payment is a payment that increases every year with inflation — the realistic way to save for a goal stated in today's rupees, or to draw a retirement income that keeps pace with prices. The trick is the inflation-adjusted (real) rate, never a plain subtraction.

Real rate r = [(1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation)] − 1 (10% and 6% → 3.7736%, NOT 4%) Serial payment for a future sum (goal in today's ₹): 1. PMT at the real rate for the goal amount in today's rupees (ordinary annuity) 2. First-year payment = PMT × (1 + inflation); each later year × (1 + inflation) again PVAD of a serial (inflation-indexed) income need: Corpus = PV of annuity DUE of today's annual need, at the real rate, for the number of years
Arjun wants ₹50,00,000 in today's money for his daughter's education in 15 years. Inflation 6%, portfolio return 10%. Real rate r = 1.10 ÷ 1.06 − 1 = 3.7736% PMT (end mode, n = 15, i = 3.7736%, FV = 50,00,000) = ₹2,53,936 (in today's ₹) First actual deposit (end of year 1) = 2,53,936 × 1.06 = ₹2,69,172, rising 6% every year Nominal goal in year 15 = 50,00,000 × 1.06¹⁵ = ₹1,19,82,791 — and the growing deposits at 10% land exactly there ✔
Suresh retires today; needs ₹6,00,000 a year in today's rupees, paid at the start of each year, for 25 years. Inflation 5%, post-retirement return 8%. Real rate = 1.08 ÷ 1.05 − 1 = 2.8571% Corpus = PVAD (BGN mode, n = 25, i = 2.8571%, PMT = 6,00,000) = ₹1,09,19,481 ≈ ₹1.09 crore If the exam says "at the end of each year" → ordinary annuity → 1,09,19,481 ÷ 1.028571 = ₹1,06,16,161
"Inflation and Serial Payments: Note on I/Y" — on the calculator, enter the real rate as a percentage with all decimals (3.7736, not 3.77). Rounding the real rate to 4% moves a 15-year answer by thousands, and FPSB puts the rounded-rate answer among the options.
Divide, don't subtract. (1+n)/(1+i) − 1. If you subtracted, your answer is always slightly too high — and that "too high" number is a distractor.
Collection: compute the real rate. Analysis: compute the first serial payment / the corpus. Synthesis: "Given a 6% inflation assumption the plan fails; which assumption change makes it feasible?" — 6-1 "impact of rate assumptions on goal achievement": a small change in return assumption over long horizons swamps a large change in the savings amount.

GIPAM Ch 4 · Assessing / comparing performance · 5-2 to 5-7

Sharpe S = (r_p − r_f) ÷ σ_p excess return per unit of TOTAL risk → rank any portfolio; use when the portfolio is the client's whole wealth / poorly diversified Treynor T = (r_p − r_f) ÷ β_p excess return per unit of SYSTEMATIC risk → use for a well-diversified portfolio or a fund that is one part of a larger diversified whole Jensen's alpha α = r_p − [ r_f + β_p (r_m − r_f) ] actual minus CAPM-required; α > 0 = manager added value Also: Information ratio = (r_p − r_b) ÷ tracking error ; M² = Sharpe-based return at market risk ; Sortino uses downside σ Rule: Sharpe and Treynor are relative (compare with benchmark's own ratio); alpha is absolute (sign tells you). If a fund's Sharpe rank ≠ Treynor rank → the difference is non-systematic risk (low R²).
Fund: return 14%, σ 16%, β 1.2. Market 12%, σ_m 15%. Risk-free 6%. Efficient frontier (Figure 14): the upper edge of the feasible set — max return for each σ. Minimum-variance portfolio at the left tip. Portfolios below/inside are dominated. Treatment rule: Cognitive biases → moderate/correct (education, data, process, checklists). Emotional biases → adapt/accommodate (adjust the allocation modestly, use nudges, framing, automation) — especially for high-wealth / low-risk-need clients. Prospect theory (Kahneman-Tversky): value is judged relative to a reference point; loss-averse (λ ≈ 2–2.5); risk-seeking in losses, risk-averse in gains; probability weighting (over-weight small probabilities). Psychology of money: money scripts from upbringing, money as security / status / freedom / love; spending to signal; the hedonic treadmill. Helping clients "behave" their way to success: written IPS as a contract with the future self; automate (SIP, auto-rebalance); pre-commitment; frame in goals not returns; reduce information noise; the planner as behavioural coach ("advisor alpha"). Choice architecture / nudges: defaults (auto-enrol), simplification, salience, timely reminders, commitment devices — nudges preserve freedom of choice.

And SGB's capital gain is exempt only on redemption with RBI (at 8 years, or early at 5/6/7 years on interest dates); selling on the exchange is a taxable transfer.
IRP = "Interest Rate Premium": rupee pays more interest, so it must be cheaper forward — the market never lets you earn both the higher coupon and the currency for free. Gold ladder for advice: ETF > SGB (if available) > gold fund > physical > digital.

Attack guide — how FPSB writes a Collection, an Analysis and a Synthesis question

Every scored item is tagged to one level of the Competency Profile. Recognising the level in the first line tells you how much reading, how much calculating and how much judgement the question wants — and where the distractors will be hiding.

Collection — "identify, calculate, list"

  • Stem is short; one fact or one formula. Options are four numbers or four terms.
  • Examples: What is the modified duration of… · Which small-savings scheme has a 21-year maturity? · Which Act created SEBI? · FV of a ₹10,000 monthly SIP for 15 years at 12%?
  • Distractors are built from the wrong mode (BGN/END), wrong compounding (annual vs monthly), the subtracted real rate, D0 instead of D1, σ instead of β, simple vs compound, price vs TRI.
  • Budget: 60–75 seconds. If you cannot see the formula in five seconds, flag and move on.

Analysis — "compare, assess, determine, distinguish"

  • Stem gives a client snippet plus two or three data points; asks which is better/more appropriate/most affected/least suitable.
  • Examples: Given TWR 9% and MWR 6%, which statement about the investor's timing is correct? · Which portfolio has the higher Sharpe… · Which forum has jurisdiction for a ₹75-lakh insurance claim? · Which bias is the client displaying when he…
  • Two options are usually true but irrelevant; one is the opposite of the right answer; one is right. Eliminate the two irrelevant ones first.
  • Budget: 90–120 seconds. Compute only what the question needs (often just the sign or the ranking).

Synthesis — "recommend, integrate, develop, prioritise"

  • Stem is a paragraph: age, goals, constraints, a behavioural cue, sometimes a regulatory wrinkle. Asks for the most appropriate action / first step / recommendation.
  • Examples: A 58-year-old with 60% in his employer's stock, retiring in two years, moderate risk, ₹40 lakh of loans… · An RIA is asked by a client to also arrange his term plan… · Curve inverted, PMI < 50, client three years from a goal…
  • The right option is the one that is consistent with every constraint in the stem — the trap options each satisfy most constraints but breach one (liquidity, tax, regulation, willingness vs ability, time horizon).
  • FPSB's ordering rule: protect first (emergency fund, insurance, debt) → then invest → then optimise (tax, estate). If an option skips a step, it is wrong.
  • Budget: 2–3 minutes; these are the questions the spare time is for.

Time plan for 75 questions in 120 minutes

  • Pass 1 (≈ 80 min): answer everything you can in under 2 minutes; flag anything longer. Never leave a blank — no negative marking.
  • Pass 2 (≈ 30 min): the flagged calculations and long Synthesis stems.
  • Pass 3 (≈ 10 min): re-read only the flagged items; do not change unflagged answers without a reason you can state.
  • Calculator: set P/Y = C/Y = 1 and 4 decimals before you start; clear TVM between questions; use the spreadsheet for IRR/NPV of uneven flows and for anything needing a table.

Formula sheet — everything numeric on one screen

Rates as decimals; n in periods; use the periodic rate for monthly flows.

Time value

  • FV = PV(1+i)^n · PV = FV/(1+i)^n · effective (1+r/m)^m − 1
  • Annuity: PV = PMT[1 − (1+i)^−n]/i · FV = PMT[(1+i)^n − 1]/i · annuity due × (1+i)
  • Perpetuity PV = PMT/i; growing PMT₁/(i − g)
  • Real rate (1+n)/(1+f) − 1; serial payment: PMT at the real rate on the today's-rupee goal, first payment × (1+f)
  • Retirement corpus (annuity due, real rate): PVAD = PMT × [1 − (1+r)^−n]/r × (1+r)
  • NPV = Σ CF/(1+k)^t − outlay; IRR: NPV = 0; Rule of 72 · 69.3 for continuous
  • EMI = P × i(1+i)^n / [(1+i)^n − 1]; outstanding = PV of remaining EMIs

Ratios

  • Front-end ≤ 28% · back-end ≤ 36% · savings ≥ 10% · emergency 3–6 months · liquidity = liquid assets/monthly expenses · debt-to-asset · net worth/total assets · solvency
  • Human Life Value = PV(income − self-consumption − tax) to retirement

Equity valuation

  • Gordon P₀ = D₁/(k − g); g = ROE × b; P/E = 1/(k−g) × payout
  • CAPM k = Rf + β(Rm − Rf); β = Cov(i,m)/σ²m = ρ σi/σm
  • Preferred P = D/k; earnings yield = EPS/P; dividend yield = DPS/P
  • FCFF → EV at WACC; equity = EV − net debt; TV = CF(1+g)/(WACC − g); EVA = NOPAT − WACC × capital
  • Cap rate: value = NOI/cap rate; NOI = GPI − vacancy − opex (before debt service, tax, depreciation)

Fixed income

  • Price = Σ C/(1+y)^t + F/(1+y)^n (semi-annual: C/2, y/2, 2n) · current yield = C/P · YTM via calculator
  • Macaulay D = Σ t × PV(CF)/P; modified D = D/(1+y/k); ΔP/P ≈ −D_mod × Δy + ½ × Convexity × Δy²
  • Immunisation: portfolio D = horizon; T-bill yield = (F−P)/P × 365/d
  • Forward/IRP: F = S × (1+i_d)/(1+i_f); futures F = S(1 + r − q)^t (or S e^{(r−q)t})

Risk and return

  • HPR = (P₁ − P₀ + income)/P₀; arithmetic vs geometric mean (geometric ≤ arithmetic); annualise (1+r)^(12/m) − 1
  • TWR = Π(1+r_sub) − 1 (manager); MWR = IRR of cash flows (investor)
  • σ_p² = w₁²σ₁² + w₂²σ₂² + 2w₁w₂ρσ₁σ₂; ρ = Cov/(σ₁σ₂); R² = ρ²; σ_p = wσ (ρ=1), |w₁σ₁ − w₂σ₂| (ρ=−1)
  • Sharpe (R−Rf)/σ · Treynor (R−Rf)/β · Jensen α = R − [Rf + β(Rm−Rf)] · Information ratio = α/tracking error · M² = Rf + Sharpe × σm · Sortino uses downside σ
  • CML: E(R) = Rf + [(E(Rm) − Rf)/σm] × σ_p (efficient portfolios only); SML: β on the x-axis (all assets)
  • Options: call intrinsic = max(S−K, 0); put = max(K−S, 0); put-call parity C + PV(K) = P + S; covered call = long stock + short call; protective put = long stock + long put
  • Margin: leverage = 1/margin; maintenance call price = P₀(1 − initial)/(1 − maintenance)

Fees and tax

  • PMS performance fee = rate × (return − hurdle) × capital above the high-water mark; catch-up fills to the full share
  • Tax-equivalent yield = tax-free yield/(1 − t); post-tax = pre-tax × (1 − t)
  • Equity MF: STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh (> 12 m); specified MF: slab; other: 12.5% after 24 m (12 m listed)

The confusions that cost marks — resolve them once, tonight

  • Balance sheet vs cash flow — stock vs flow; net worth is on the balance sheet, savings ratio on the cash-flow statement.
  • Front-end vs back-end — housing only (28) vs all debt (36).
  • Annuity due vs ordinary — rent, SIP-at-start, retirement withdrawals at the beginning of the month are due.
  • Nominal vs real vs effective — three different rates; the serial-payment and retirement questions use real.
  • NPV vs IRR — NPV assumes reinvestment at k, IRR at IRR; conflicts → NPV wins.
  • Macaulay vs modified vs effective duration — years vs price sensitivity vs option-adjusted.
  • Coupon > YTM → premium; coupon < YTM → discount; current yield sits between coupon rate and YTM.
  • Systematic vs unsystematic — β vs residual; diversification removes only the second.
  • CML vs SML — σ vs β on the x-axis; CML only for efficient portfolios.
  • Sharpe vs Treynor vs Jensen — σ / β / α; M² for comparison with the market.
  • TWR vs MWR — manager vs investor; MWR < TWR means the investor added money before a fall.
  • Arithmetic vs geometric — forecast vs history; geometric is lower.
  • Strategic vs tactical vs dynamic allocation — policy weights / short-term tilts / rules-based response.
  • Calendar vs threshold rebalancing — time vs drift band.
  • Anchoring vs conservatism vs status quo — a number / slow updating / inertia.
  • Loss aversion vs regret aversion vs disposition — pain of loss / fear of a wrong act / sell winners hold losers.
  • Framing vs mental accounting — how the question is put / which bucket the money sits in.
