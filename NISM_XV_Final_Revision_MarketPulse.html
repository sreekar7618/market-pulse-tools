What an RA is, and the three kinds of analyst

Chapter 1.1

Research = collecting information from reliable sources. Analysis = processing it into a buy / hold / sell decision. The workbook calls the RA a selector. Legally, an RA is a person who, for consideration, provides research services, including part-time RAs.

The workbook's phone analogy: shortlist brands in your budget, compare battery and camera specs, decide. That is research + analysis. Enterprise value and the price multiples

Chapter 3.1.7, 3.1.11–3.1.14 · sample question

EV = Market cap + preference + debt (+ minority, consolidated) − cash − non-operating investments
10 lakh shares at ₹340; preferred ₹8.5 cr, debt ₹6.4 cr (book value as proxy), cash ₹2.5 cr, investments ₹1.4 cr. Mcap = 340 × 10,00,000 = ₹34.0 cr EV = 34.0 + 8.5 + 6.4 − 2.5 − 1.4 = ₹45.0 cr Sample Q: Mcap 10 L, debt 3 L, cash 4 L → EV = 9 L
EV is the price of buying the whole business: pay the shareholders, take over the debt, keep the spare cash. So subtract anything that doesn't run the business — cash, FDs, liquid funds, surplus investments. Receivables and inventory stay in (they're operating assets).

Persistent CAD weakens the currency unless funded by capital inflows; a weaker currency makes imports dearer, exports cheaper. Surpluses build confidence; deficits lose it only when they aren't funded.

"Deficit needs a donor": a CAD must be funded by inflows (donors) or by reserves (savings), and neither lasts forever. Confidence is lost when the gap can't be filled, not when there is a surplus. A deficit funded by FDI holds; one funded by hot FPI money is fragile.

Porter's Five Forces

Chapter 6.6.1 · near-certain MCQ

Horizontal (3)Vertical (2)
Threat of substitutes · Threat of new entrants · Rivalry among existing firmsBargaining power of suppliers · Bargaining power of buyers

Rivalry is high with many firms, little differentiation, price-only strategies and low switching costs. Unattractive: aviation, telecom, retail, textile, sugar, power. Attractive: education, FMCG, healthcare, IT.

Education is the workbook's attractive industry: rising demand, weak student bargaining power, high entry barriers (permissions), few quality rivals, salaries set by management (weak suppliers), few trusted substitutes.
Buffett: when brilliant management meets a business with bad economics, it's the business's reputation that stays intact. Munger: if price is the only basis of competition, it's a self-defeating business.
Buffett looks for "economic castles" protected by unbreachable "moats" — high entry barriers that give pricing power, so the business can charge a premium without losing customers.
Suppliers and buyers are the vertical forces. Porter published the model in 1979.

Risks, credit rating, ESG, sources

Chapter 7.7–7.10

Risks: business, financial, key-man, concentration, regulatory, technology. Credit rating = external view of debt-servicing ability; a downgrade raises funding cost. ESG = environmental, social and governance factors as long-term risk and opportunity.

Promoter holds 60% and has pledged half. A sharp price fall lets lenders sell the pledged shares, which drives the price lower and can trigger more selling. Heavy pledging is both a governance and a price-risk flag.
High pledge + falling price = forced selling. Pledging alone is routine fund-raising; it's the high percentage that hurts. ESG is a filter, not a buy signal — BRSR disclosures are mandatory for the top 1,000 listed companies from FY2023. Promoters who say "nothing can go wrong" don't know what they don't know — avoid them.

Sources: annual reports, quarterly results, exchange filings, management meetings, plant visits, experts, suppliers, customers.

Interest rate risk: rates up → bond prices down. Reinvestment risk: rates down → coupons reinvested lower. Call risk: issuer redeems early. The workbook's printed standard deviation formula (p 238) omits the square root, which gives variance. Sample SD = √[Σ(x − mean)² ÷ (n − 1)]. Use the correct form when calculating.

VaR(1%) = 12% reads as: 99% confident the loss won't exceed 12%. Confidence = 1 − significance. Risk is known uncertainty you can measure; uncertainty is when you don't yet know the cause.

Hammer and hanging man are the same candle; the location decides. A man is hanged at the top — bad news, bearish. A hammer hits the bottom to build a base — bullish. Both need the next candle to confirm.
A reversal is named after where the price is going, not where it came from: at a top it's bearish, at a bottom it's bullish. The shape gives the family, the prior trend gives the name — long lower shadow: hammer ↔ hanging man; long upper shadow: inverted hammer ↔ shooting star.
Engulfing patterns compare real bodies only, not wicks. A piercing pattern is two candles at the bottom, not three at the top.

Moving averages and MACD

Chapter 15.9.1–15.9.2

Moving averages (simple or exponential) smooth noise and lag price; price above a rising MA = uptrend.

Fast MACD = difference of the 12- and 26-period EMAs Slow MACD = 9-period EMA of the fast line (not bounded 0–100)
  • With both lines rising, buy when fast crosses above slow (reverse for sell).
  • A zero-line crossover should not be used for trading.
  • Steep = powerful move; lines holding a steady gap = trend in place; a bulge = buying climax.
  • MACD double top/bottom → reversal likely. Avoid stocks with weekly or monthly MACD trending down.
  • Shows momentum, not price — use with other indicators.
The workbook words the fast line as "26 EMA − 12 EMA". The standard construction is 12 EMA − 26 EMA; the signal line (9 EMA) is the same either way. If a question tests the order, go with the workbook's wording.
